```
A confession: every Python-for-data course I've taught has had a moment where I
realised the curriculum I was teaching from didn't quite match the job I was
preparing students for.

The courses taught Python. The job needed Python + pandas + statistics + ML
+ LLMs + a packaging discipline that survives 3 a.m. failures. So I started
building the curriculum I wished existed.

Today I'm sharing v2 of it.

  📓  7 modules · 25 notebooks · 11 optional appendices
  🧪  180+ exercises with detailed worked solutions
  🏆  Two capstones — analytical + engineering
  💻  Runs entirely offline thanks to a built-in MockLLM
  🗺️  Five learning paths from "curious manager" to "complete beginner"

A few choices that may be different from courses you've seen:

▪️ Practice exercises *plus* Stretch exercises (A–D) in every notebook.
  Practice tests the concept while it's fresh. Stretch is the harder question
  you'd want to answer in an interview. Both have full solutions with a
  Reasoning paragraph — knowing why an answer works matters more than the
  answer itself.

▪️ The AI-engineering notebooks (RAG, agents, document processing, eval)
  ship with an offline MockLLM. You learn the patterns without paying for
  tokens. When you're ready, swap a single import for OpenAI, Anthropic,
  Google, or Ollama — the rest of the notebook stays identical.

▪️ A 🐞 "Debug-me" cell in every foundations notebook. It deliberately errors.
  You read the traceback, fix the bug, learn the habit. Reading errors
  fluently is the single most underrated skill of working programmers.

▪️ A 🎁 bonus mini-project at the end of every chapter. The chapter's ideas
  leave the playground and meet a realistic scenario — KPI dashboards,
  ETL pipelines, scheduled jobs, retrieval queues.

▪️ A course-overview notebook with per-notebook time budgets and an
  interactive estimator. You enter the notebooks you plan to do and it
  tells you "≈ 27 hours total — at 3 h/week that's 9 weeks." The shrinking
  remaining-time number is surprisingly motivating.

The course is free. If you teach Python or data science, supervise newcomers,
or are learning this stack yourself, take a look. I'd particularly love to
hear which sections landed and which ones could use another pass — every
honest critique makes the next revision better.

Repo + start-here notebook in the comments.

#Python #DataScience #MachineLearning #AIEngineering #LLMs #BusinessAnalytics
#Teaching #DataLiteracy #HigherEducation
```

## Suggested customisations

- **Bold sentences** — LinkedIn renders `**bold**` as bold. Use sparingly on the opening line and one or two key claims.
- **Hashtags** — keep ≤ 5 per post for best reach (LinkedIn punishes longer lists). The ones above are starting points; pick the 3–5 that match your network.
- **Link placement** — the "link in the comments" trick reliably outperforms a link inside the post itself, because LinkedIn's algorithm down-ranks posts that send users off-platform.
- **Image** — adding the 7-module ASCII diagram from `00b_course_overview.ipynb` as a screenshot doubles engagement on technical posts.
- **Tagging** — if there are colleagues at HSBI who contributed or who'd want to share, tag them.
